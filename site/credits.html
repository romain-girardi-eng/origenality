This map is made of other people's cataloguing. The libraries and projects below did the work of describing the scholarship on Origen; what is added here is an arrangement of their metadata and a vocabulary for reading it.

Romain Girardi · August 2026

Where the project comes from

At the closing round table of Origeniana XIV, in Boston on 14 August 2026, a participant asked the question the field asks itself: how does one say anything new about Origen, given the mass of what has been written and the impossibility of reading it all. Origenality is one answer, proposed publicly that afternoon — map the ground already covered, so that the uncovered ground can be seen. It is a doctoral side project, kept deliberately modest in scope: metadata, not full text.

Summaries: credit, and removal on request

A summary is someone's prose. It is shown with their name on it, and it comes down when they ask.

Every summary displayed in the Explorer names the database that wrote it and links to the record there. 381 of the 1 632 mapped records carry one. 166 were written by IxTheo itself and 215 by another database that describes the same publication: OpenAlex, GIROTA / Adamantius, ISIDORE (Huma-Num), Crossref, Semantic Scholar. A summary written for this project, where one exists, says so instead of naming a database.

How to have summaries removed. Write to romain.girardi@univ-cotedazur.fr, naming the database, the imprint or the article. No justification is needed and none will be asked for. The summaries come off the site and out of the next data release, and the record keeps its bibliographic metadata and its link, so nothing is lost to the reader but the paragraph. Requests are answered as they arrive, and each one is logged in the project's data policy with its date and its scope.

The partner databases were written to on 15 August 2026, before any of this went up: BIBP at Laval, GIROTA for the Adamantius repertorio, and IxTheo at Tübingen. The same offer stands for any publisher or author who finds their prose here.

Reference works the vocabulary leans on

  • Clavis Origenis, ed. Samuel Fernández and Alfons Fürst (Adamantiana 30, Münster, 2024): the register of Origen's works, their sigla and their transmission, behind the thirty-three identifiers of the works axis.
  • Adamantius, repertorio bibliografico (GIROTA): its published sections gave the axis of author and milieu in the theme vocabulary.
  • GND and VIAF authority files, for the identity of Origen and for the guard against the homonym Origenes Platonicus.
  • BiblIndex (Lyon): not a source of records here, but a standing precedent for what a reference index of patristic citation can be.

  • The landing portrait is an evocation of Origen of Alexandria, not a likeness. No ancient portrait of him survives to copy.

Author and standing

Origenality is written and maintained by Romain Girardi, doctoral candidate, University of Geneva. Sole author of the site, the vocabulary and the code.

The project is academic and non-commercial. It cites rather than replaces: every record links back to the catalogue that describes it, and the point of the exercise is to send readers to the work of others.

Corrections are welcome, particularly on the vocabulary. A term that is wrong in a controlled vocabulary is wrong on every record that carries it, which is a good reason to publish the vocabulary rather than hide it.
